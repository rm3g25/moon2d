# LORE.md

The canon of the Moon 2D universe. Source of truth for all story text in
the game. Part of this document describes the wider universe ("Dark
Planet 1999", the Order of Elementals) — none of that is **ever revealed**
in Moon 2D; it lives here so the game does not contradict future games.
For what the player sees and what stays hidden, see "Disclosure".

Russian version: `LORE-ru.md`.

---

## 1. Place in the universe

Moon 2D is a **prequel** to "Dark Planet 1999". The events at the TEK
lunar base give birth to fast-travel technology, thanks to which the
heroes of "Dark Planet" will later reach the planets of the nearest star
and end up on the Dark Planet itself.

Timeline: Moon 2D → (years of TEK's transit program) →
"Dark Planet 1999".

## 2. The TEK corporation

An Earth megacorporation and monopolist (inspiration: UAC from Doom,
Weyland-Yutani from Aliens — minus the cartoonish evil). TEK is not a
villain: it simply values assets above people. The research must be
saved; who survives in the process is a secondary question.

The TEK lunar research base sits on the **far side of the Moon**. Its
profile: terraforming the Moon (the oxygen plant, atmospheric
facilities), ore mining, and — the crown secret — Project TRANSIT.

TEK's signature style: the protocol is sacred. Quarantine, TEK-R1
security robots, orbital automation — everything runs by the book even
when the book kills its own people.

## 3. The model of the worlds: crumpled sheets

Our reality and the parallel one are two crumpled sheets of paper
pressed against each other. Between them lies a **membrane**. The sheets
do not match geometrically: they are different universes with no
point-over-point coordinate grid between them.

The sheets touch only in certain places — **nodes**, where the membrane
is thin. The key property: **the correspondence between the worlds does
not preserve distance**. Two nodes three kilometers apart on the other
side may open into points three hundred kilometers apart on ours. The
sheets are crumpled, and crumpled differently.

Fast travel is a wormhole not through space but through a parallel
reality: dive into node A, walk across the other sheet, emerge from
node B. Our geometry is left standing there looking silly.

At the scale of "Dark Planet" the same principle leads to the stars:
distance in our own space is not the argument; where the nodes are — is.

## 4. Selene (the Anti-Moon)

The parallel world is a mirror inversion of our pair: over there the
**Moon is alive and the Earth is dead**. The base scientists call that
side "Selene". Why the inversion exists is never explained by anyone —
it is a mystery of the world.

Selene is a Moon of ordinary size, but with a biosphere of **mineral
life**: living rock, breathing stone. This is where the game's creatures
come from — gravels, betoners and the rest. They are not an invasion
army; they are fauna pouring through an open door.

## 5. Project TRANSIT (Проект «ПЕРЕХОД»)

The base scientists discovered the membrane and the natural nodes,
learned to **thin the membrane**, and mapped the nodes on both sides.
The **Rift** is their first forcibly opened node, located in the base's
laboratory complex.

The catastrophe (inspiration: Half-Life): the cascade during the opening
went off-spec. The Rift stabilized in the open state, and energy and
Selenean fauna poured through. No sabotage, no "creatures gnawing a
hole" — people opened the door themselves and could not close it.

### Timeline of the catastrophe
1. The Rift is opened. An energy surge; cave-ins in the far sections of
   the base — including the route to the transmitter complex.
2. Selenean fauna emerges from the Rift. The personnel die.
3. The Rift's energy jams the airwaves: transmitters physically cannot
   punch a signal through that background. The link to Earth and the
   base's internal network fall at the same time.
4. The friend-or-foe network is dead → per quarantine protocol the
   TEK-R1 robots switch to "everything that moves is a target". They
   have not gone mad; they are faithfully following the manual.
5. TEK's orbital automation holds the quarantine: nothing leaves the far
   side until the main transmitter sends the all-clear code. Which it
   cannot send. See item 3. The trap snaps shut.

## 6. The hero

Officially: a **lieutenant of the TEK armed forces**, sent on a shuttle
with several scientists to find out why the base has gone silent and to
bring a transmitter back online so the research data can be relayed to
Earth.

Secretly (never revealed in the game): the hero is a member of the
**Order of Elementals**, planted inside the expedition. The Order's
council sensed the Rift — a hole between realities lies squarely in
their domain — and placed their own man inside the corporate mission.
This is why one soldier survives where the entire base fell.

The shuttle scientists stay at the spaceport with the ship. Possible
story use: radio voices in the level texts. The second story vehicle is
the logs of the dead base personnel (System Shock style): a game with no
NPCs, the texts do all the work.

## 7. The Order of Elementals — DO NOT REVEAL IN GAME

Wider-universe context. In Moon 2D: zero mentions.

- A future order on Earth, keepers of the peace, with its own council
  (inspiration: the Jedi).
- **Object control**: an elemental enters a trance state and moves
  things. The mechanic is not force but choice: in the meta-space of
  variants (inspiration: Zeland) he selects the reality in which the
  object has been moved. Only an **adjacent** variant can be selected —
  a reality differing by a hair; the variant "the object is already
  across the room" is too far away in probability, out of reach. So the
  elemental slides along a chain of neighboring variants, which from the
  outside looks like an object drifting through the air. Telekinesis as
  an enumeration of realities in millimeter steps.
- **The element and the belt** (inspiration: Kamen Rider): every
  elemental carries his own element with him (in a pocket). Inserted
  into the belt, the element amplifies his abilities: the elemental
  moves scraps of matter around himself, heating them and collapsing
  them onto his body into a suit — the **henshin**.
- The henshin bonus in Moon 2D is exactly this, but the game presents it
  simply as a bonus, with no explanation.

## 8. Monsters

Selenean fauna is mineral life. Gravels (gravel, gravelFemale) are
creatures of gravel; betoners (betoner) are of sintered rock. The rest
of the bestiary (winter, zombieShooter, etc.) should, as needed, be
written in as forms of the same mineral biosphere or as victims of its
influence.

The security system (turret, TEK-R1, boss1) is the second faction of
threats: TEK machines running the quarantine protocol.

The final monster (level 10): a **warden from Selene**, who came not by
accident — it wants to close the Rift from its own side. The hero is,
in essence, fighting it for the right to keep the door ajar. Morally
ambiguous; it hints that in "Dark Planet" humanity is walking through
someone else's house.

## 9. Level layout (10)

Dramatic arc: downward → through the mirror → surfacing at the antenna.

1. **Spaceport** — the base's transport hub. (done)
2. **Tunnels** — service tunnels, the oxygen plant, Section A.
   Everything runs, nobody's home. Ends with the gravel trial. (done)
3. **Residential sector** — quarters, mess hall, greenhouse. An empty
   home is scarier than an empty factory. Expands the "residential
   mini-complex" sign from level 1. Mechanically gentle — the entry
   point into Phase 2.
4. **Ore train** — the ore transporter, the road down to the mines and
   the labs. A large train: interior plus roof sections.
5. **Reactor** — emergency mode. Without power the Rift cannot be
   stabilized and the crossing cannot be survived.
6. **TRANSIT laboratories** — the epicenter of the catastrophe,
   scientists' logs, the node map, the entrance to the Rift. The hero
   enters out of desperation: the direct route to the transmitter is
   buried, the Rift is the only corridor. The first controlled fast
   travel in history. On foot.
7. **Selene: surface** — the living Moon; a dead Earth hangs in the sky.
8. **Selene: depths** — the heart of the living rock. Here the hero
   retrieves the **resonator** from TRANSIT's automated field camp
   (deployed there before the catastrophe). The resonator opens a node
   from the far side — without it a natural node is just a wall.
9. **Selene: exodus** — the road to the exit node. Something big has
   realized its house has been broken into. A chase; dimension-shift.
10. **Transmitter complex** — the exit node leads straight here
    (crumpled sheets: three kilometers there — hundreds here). The
    densest security, the warden that followed him through. The final
    battle, the data and the all-clear code sent to Earth, credits.

## 10. Finale and the setup

The research data and the resonator's parameters go to Earth, the
quarantine is lifted. The Rift is **not closed**: TEK takes it under
control as "Gate-1" — the prototype of the transit program. The door
creaks, it is dark behind it, the corporation rubs its hands. Years
later this program leads people to the nearest star — to the events of
"Dark Planet 1999".

TEK's press release will rewrite the hero's trek as "a planned
controlled-crossing experiment". Naturally.

## 11. Disclosure: what the player sees and what stays hidden

**The player learns:** the TEK base has gone silent; the hero is a TEK
lieutenant; the scientists opened a Rift into a parallel world and died;
the robots are on quarantine; the road to the transmitter runs through
Selene; the resonator; the signal is sent.

**The player does NOT learn:** the elementals, the Order, the council,
the trance state, the space of variants, the nature of the henshin, why
the worlds are inverted, the further fate of Gate-1. All of that belongs
to other games of the universe.
