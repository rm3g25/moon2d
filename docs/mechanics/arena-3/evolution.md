# Arena 3: the evolution, the throw, the takeoff, the orb launcher, the barge

From the bunker's death to the hero aboard the barge under the hanging Ram: the last 3%, the eclipse rite, the burst, the throw of the pads, the wheels, the takeoff, the orb launcher, the cliff and the barge. The air fight that follows is `ram-air.md`.

## Evolution and the moon form
- Evolution: black orbs fly to the hero - the henshin rite (docs/mechanics/henshin.md) with black orbs and a third pattern, "eclipse": a black ring closing in (snowflake for arena 1, vortex for the gravels, eclipse here). Healing as the rite heals.
- The moon form: an orb launcher - black orbs that pierce the TEK armour; jump +25%, as the ice form; the fire rain and the protective aura, both of black orbs.
- The look of the moon form comes in the next version; in 3.0 it looks like the ice form. It must be felt through the weapon: the orb launcher fires slower but heavier, with visible recoil and sound - a sledgehammer after a pistol.
- Death after the evolution: the fight restarts from its start (as every death here).

## The henshin, the throw and the takeoff (designed 2026-11-04)
Over "Evolution and the moon form", "Air phase" and "Decided 2026-11-01, the fight in motion" where they disagree: the world stops for the henshin and the throw, and the fight goes on in motion again from the takeoff. Three parts: the last of the charge, the rite, the scene with the Ram.

### The heartbeat
- One beat runs from 97% to the throw. The gauge pulses with it, the heart monitor on the HUD beats in step, the orbs of the rite come in on it, the ring of the rite beats on it, the burst is its strongest beat, the pads spin up as it quickens. Silence after the throw.
- A double "tuk-tuk", low - synthesized in code under `tools/sounds/` as the stone's drone (low sounds work in code, clicks and crackles do not); replaceable by a file.
- First guesses: 60 beats a minute at 97%, 90 at 100%, 140 at the burst; 70 to 150 through the spin of the pads.

### The last 3%
- The bunker dead, the Ram hunts on as built (3.0.98), and the last percents come as built - quick: nothing happens and it is easy to dodge, a breather.
- The gauge leaves its slot on the heart monitor's panel and rides to the top centre, where the level's title appears, growing on the way (first guess: three times its size in the slot). It beats with the heartbeat; the number in it keeps counting. The slot stays empty: the gauge is gone for good after the eclipse.

### 100%: the brake and the eclipse
- **The world stops before the rite.** The Ram drives the screen's motion, so at 100 it brakes - the hiss and the jolt - and the belt runs down with it (the run-down of 11a): the far plane, the fangs and the pads come to rest. Nothing moves to pull the eye off the scene. A hero inside the hull's zone at that moment is set out of it with no wound. From the brake to the wheel nothing wounds him.
- **The order, decided 2026-11-05** (over the eclipse of the disc, its corona, the diamond ring and the beam out of the diamond, built in 3.0.101 and taken out in 3.0.102: a beam out of a point in the HUD reads as a laser, not as light from above). The world at rest, the orbs come and lift him and circle him on the heartbeat; then the light goes; then, in the dark, the beam falls from above - the column of light of the Tron Legacy poster.
- **The control is taken** as the world comes to rest, and given back at the wheel. The orbs catch the hero where he is - on a pad, on the road, in the air - and lift him a cell; at an edge of the screen or by the hull he is drawn clear first. He does not touch the ground till the wheel: the rite, the beam, the burst and the throw are one levitation. *Replaced 2026-11-06: the hero runs, jumps and shoots through the rite, the beam and the burst - a paralysis from the brake to the wheel is too long; the catch and the lift move to the throw (18a), and the control comes back at the wheel as before. See "Stage 16d reworked".*
- **The light goes** once he is up: one overlay darkens the far plane and the play layer, the backdrop's sun with the rest; over it stay the beam, the hero, the orbs and the Ram's red eye. The gauge at the top centre goes out with the light, for good. The darkness is the rite's: in 16d it deepens as the orbs gather.
- **The beam falls from above**: a vertical column from the top edge of the screen straight down onto him, wherever he hangs - a white core about his width in a wide moonlit glow, soft edges, the light running out below his feet; a flash as it lands, a halo round him, and it swells and brightens on each heartbeat.
- **The boss track** fades out to silence as the light goes - paused, not stopped; the heartbeat alone.

### The rite
- Black orbs, one at a time, fast, from all sides: from the road, the pads, the fangs, the hull, and part of them from the far city - a point deep in the far plane that grows as it comes, out of the depth into the pattern. The gaps shorten with the beat (first guess: 30 orbs, 0.35 s down to 0.06 s, about 4 s).
- **The pattern "eclipse"**: a ring round the hero that beats. On each double beat it draws in and breathes out, and a faint ripple of light runs from it over the dark world. Not a heart shape - the heart is the beat, not the figure. A third pattern beside the snowflake and the vortex (`Orbs.Patterns`).
- The orbs wound nothing: there is no enemy but the Ram, and it takes no damage yet. The rite's healing: +3 at its end.
- The collapse over the whole body, as the rite does it; the body fills with light. The form becomes the moon form (`hfMoon`): in 3.0 it looks like the ice form and jumps +25% as it does - the wheel needs that jump.

### The burst
- Stronger than in arenas 1 and 2. The last, strongest beat, the inhale (half a second of silence), the strike: more rays, longer, across the screen and off it, through the far plane; a white core with the black tail and light halo of the moon look. The screen flashes white for two frames; the darkness is torn off in one frame - the light is back; the shake.
- The Ram is thrown back half a cell and stunned: its eye flickers out of step, its lamps go out, smoke from the wells. The rays wound nothing.

### The throw
- The hero hangs on his orbs. From the points of his body threads of orbs go out to the pads on the screen - whatever pads stand there, his own included; too few or too many is tuned after the live run.
- The pads' jets die - the orbs hold them now. They rise a little and spin about their own axis, slowly at first, faster with the beat, under a rising hum. The Ram's honeycomb lights up in alarm.
- Spun up, the hint appears over the hero: the mouse-button glyph of the bonus hint (3.0.5), pulsing with the beat. A press throws them; with no press they go by themselves in 3 s.
- Almost at once: each pad lets go at its own small delay (first guess 0 to 0.2 s) and flies, spinning, engines burning (the wrecks' flight of 10c-3), at the honeycomb. Each hit a blast and a piece of the honeycomb shattered, the last a big blast; then the rest of the honeycomb blinks and goes out cell by cell from the hits outward.
- **Decided 2026-11-06, after the live run of the first build of 18a-1** (over the points above where they disagree): threads of orbs from the hero to the pads read as a spider's web. The orbs come out of his body and fly off him to the pads and cling to their beams, and so they spin them. The pad spins about the middle of its beam, not of its picture (the engines hang under the beam). The Ram drives off at once after the burst, to the left third of the screen. The hero is carried out of the picture - a little over the road by the right edge. The gripped pads glide slowly to places on an arc round the Ram's right side, all as far from it, evenly apart - from the arc the volley reaches it all at once. The orbs are too big against the ice form's - left so for now, a step of its own later. *After the second run, the same day*: the Ram drives off farther, leaving a cell of road behind it; the arc wider, its top just under the top of the screen - room for the pads to gather speed at the volley. *After the third run*: the honeycomb does not flash in alarm - the Ram is not afraid; its lamps and its eye blink.
- **The music after the honeycomb (decided 2026-11-06).** The silence lasts from the volley to the last hit; with the big blast the music starts - from its beginning, fading in, not where it paused. For now the boss track; a new track of its own is planned for this moment - the fight after the armour, from the wheel on - and replaces it once it is made. *Made (2026-11-07), `flight.ogg`, in since 3.0.115 - see "The flight's track as built".*

### The wheel
- The armour gone, the front wheel - the one toward the hero, the hull facing right since the hunt - breaks off. The hull drops on that side and scrapes the road: sparks, a scrape. The wheel bounces once and rolls right, at the hero.
- The hero comes down to the road as the wheel is let go, and the control is his again. The wheel wounds as the hull's touch does and is to be jumped - sized for the moon form's jump. It rolls off the right edge.
- **Decided 2026-11-06, before the build of 18b** (over the points above where they disagree): the wheel keeps its own size, 41 - the very wheel off the axle; any jump clears it (the plain jump climbs 100, the moon form's 155), and the moon form's jump waits for the pads over the abyss of 18d. From the wheel on, the hull's touch wounds and throws again, as before the henshin: the control back, the fight back. The bow's plough stands on the road already, so the hull has nowhere to drop: it leans a few degrees about its rear wheel's touch and the point of the plough digs under the road line - the nose dug in.

### Stage 16b as built (3.0.100)
- **The heartbeat** (`Game.Heartbeat`, `THeartbeat`): it counts the beats and knows no charge - the game hands it a tempo every tick (`Tick`), so the later steps of the henshin can drive it by their own clock. The first tick after a stop strikes at once; then the tempo is gathered tick by tick and a beat is struck once a minute's worth of ticks is in, so a change of tempo keeps the phase. `Struck` - the first thump this tick; `Pulse(alpha)` - both thumps, the second `DubTicks` 8 (0.24 s) later at 0.7, each fading over 7 ticks. `ChargeTempo`: 60 a minute at `HeartbeatCharge` 97, 90 at full, straight between.
- **When it beats** (`TMoonGame.TickHeartbeat`, after the gauge's tick): past the bunker (`FPastBunker`) with the charge at 97 or more; otherwise still. A restart and a new level stop it, so point A starts it again with a beat. Each beat plays `heartbeat.wav` and beats the monitor.
- **The sound** (`tools/sounds/heartbeat.py`, `heartbeat.wav`, 0.6 s - shorter than a beat at 90): two dull thumps falling from 70 to 45 Hz with two overtones, a soft attack, under a low-pass at 220 Hz; the second 0.24 s on, quieter and a little higher. `DubSeconds` there and `DubTicks` in the code are one number.
- **The ride** (`TGaugeRide`, `Hud.Energy`): `Lift` from the slot, `Dock` back at once. 66 ticks, eased in and out (smoothstep), from the slot to the middle of the level's title line (`BigMessageY` of `Hud.Messages`, now public, plus half a big glyph; the middle of the frame across), growing to three times its size on the same curve; it stays up. The whole gauge draws at the ride's centre and scale - the disc, the halo, the rim, the arc and its head, the number and its sign. The slot stays open and empty: `WingWidth` follows the charge alone. `Heart` is still the slot, where the stone's orbs fly. Lifted by the bunker's blast and by `ResumePastBunker` - point A rides out of the slot again; docked by `RestartLevel` and the End key's level reset.
