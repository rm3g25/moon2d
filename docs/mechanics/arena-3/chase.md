# Arena 3: the chase (act 2, turret 3)

The belt, the far plane and the city, the fangs, the drifting pads, the bunker in three phases, up to the bunker's death.

### Act 2 - the chase (turret 3)
Turret 2 dies: turret 3 comes out, a napalm launcher opens on the stern, and the machine drives off to the right. It does not leave the screen - the screen moves under it.

**The belt.** The screen becomes a treadmill the hero does not steer:
- the floor slides left; the machine stays on the right part of the screen, its wheels turning;
- the backdrop stands still (decided 2026-10-20): a seamless backdrop is not going to be made, and it does not have to be - the far wall of a giant tunnel would barely move anyway, as distant mountains from a car window. The speed is shown by the floor, the things of the world riding it and the wheels;
- everything that belongs to the world - the hero, the napalm puddles, the pads, the medkits - is carried left at the belt's speed, in the air as on the floor (otherwise a jump would be a free way to stand still);
- an idle hero drifts back to the left wall, so the hero has to run to keep up. The machine is slow, the hero is faster: running, he gains on the belt at about the belt's own speed;
- the collision grid does not move: the floor is flat and the same along its length, so only the picture slides. The floor is a row of tiles - a classic square sprite, so sliding it is a tile offset that wraps, nothing to stitch;
- the fangs ride through from right to left at random - separate sprites, nothing to loop - "posts by the road" that sell the speed (decided 2026-10-21, see "The fangs");
- the belt comes up to speed from a standstill and runs down to one at the end - no jerk either way.

**The napalm launcher - why the hero runs.** An unkillable launcher on the machine's stern (part of the armoured hull: hits on it flare the honeycomb):
- fires almost straight up; the shell climbs off the top of the screen;
- it falls straight down behind the hero: it is aimed at where the hero was about a second ago - a running hero leaves it behind, a standing one gets it on his head;
- the shells land in the fire zone only - from the left wall to the fire's edge. The edge is unstable: it breathes, and now and then lurches a couple of cells toward the middle for a few seconds, narrowing the room. More often as turret 3 loses lives;
- every shell leaves a burning puddle that lives a few seconds and rides the belt to the wall. The wall of fire at the left is not an effect of its own - it is the puddles piling up there.
- The look, in four beats: the launch (a flash at the stern, a thick orange tracer with a smoke trail climbing out of sight, a dull thump); the wait (about 0.7 s: a shimmer or a red flicker on the floor where it will land, a whistle growing from above - the warning); the drop (a blob of napalm with a ragged dripping trail); the splash (tongues of fire to the sides, then the puddle, a loop of a few frames).
- Contact with the falling drop or a puddle wounds the hero.

**Turret 3, the bunker - hit from above only.** Shielded on its sides and front by the honeycomb, open on top: a hatch. The hero aims with the mouse in any direction, so from a pad above the machine he shoots down into the hatch. From the floor every shot meets the shield. Its fire (decided 2026-10-26): it turns to the hero and fires a volley that drops to the floor and rolls along it, so the floor is no shelter and the pads are the way; the hatch is shut for the volley and open about 1.5 s after it - the window to strike.

**The drifting pads.** Pads on the upper layer, standing still in the world, so on the screen they drift left with the floor:
- a pad carries whoever stands on it toward the fire, as the floor does: a pad is not a rest but a stepping stone - cross it to the next one;
- a ladder of heights: low pads reachable from the floor with the plain jump, higher ones, and the height of the hatch;
- the pattern is random (decided), but a stretch that leads up to the hatch comes at least every few seconds, so the player is never left with nothing to climb.

**Medkits on the upper layer.** A medkit rides the upper layer with the pads and drifts past: climbed up and took it - good; did not - it is gone off the left edge.

**The brake lurch.** Now and then the machine brakes hard and rolls back about a cell - in the chase it backs, so "back" is its ram, toward the hero. The warning: its eye blinks fast for about half a second (2026-10-26; the brake lights face away from the hero in the chase). Whoever it meets is wounded and shoved by the contact rule (decided 2026-10-19 as a crush). With the fire behind, jumping clear of the bumper may land in the napalm - check live that it is not too much.

**Decided 2026-10-30, the chase, over the text above where they disagree:**
- **A quiet danger on the left.** No wall of fire: a screen that burns for the whole act is noise. The mortar is a leash - it fires only while the hero is in the left third of the screen (the zone grows by the bunker's thirds, unseen); a hero who keeps up sees a quiet road. A puddle lives about 1.5 s - the splash, a short burn - then it is soot that smokes and rides the belt away. The left edge itself carries only a heat haze and a dark red vignette that deepens as the hero comes near it: no flames.
- **The backdrop goes from the tunnels to the city - the city comes almost at once.** A fast player must not miss it: the tunnel ends within the first seconds of the chase, then the city is long. The far plane starts fast (the machine speeds up) and slows along the city; the evolution and the air phase then play on a still screen in front of it, so everyone sees the city.
- **The city.** In a vast crater beyond the end of the tunnel: abandoned, intact, not ruined - everyone died or left. Retro-futuristic, synthwave: white and chrome spires, domed halls, stepped ziggurats, ring roads, a monorail on pylons; most windows dark, a few pink and cyan neon signs still lit, one may flicker. Under a gigantic dome that is only its frame - ribs and rings, no glass. Big: the next level goes through it. A main dome (the city) and a smaller satellite dome (industry: cooling towers, tanks, cranes, masts), the monorail between them.
- **The convoys** are autonomous TEK machinery going about some business of their own; what it is, is left for later. They stop coming where the strata end.
- **The sun** stands still on the screen - it is infinitely far - so it is not on the backdrop but a layer of its own drawn in code (disc, halo, glare), behind the backdrop. The backdrop's sky is transparent: the sun shows through it only where the rock has ended, so it comes out from behind the cliff by itself. Placed low, the far ridge cuts its lower part - a sunset with no code for it. The light is white and hard while nothing stands between it and the hero; where a dome stands in front of it the code tints it pink-orange (the dome's span along the strip is in the data) - the synthwave sunset is born on the move. Stars, if any, are code too.
- **The backdrop is built from elements, not drawn on in one strip.** ChatGPT redraws the whole picture when asked to continue one, and the kept half drifts - on the ribs of a dome a seam shows. So every object is one generation on a clear ground, whole, and nothing is ever seamed through it: the cliff (the end of the tunnel, joined to the wall of screen 13), the far ridge and the crater floor (uniform textures, made seamless by code), the main dome with its city, the satellite dome, the small things (a radio dish, a hangar, a landing ring, monorail spans). One style reference for every generation. The layers may move at speeds of their own - a parallax inside the far plane. The strip, its layers and speeds, the sun, the prompts and the tools: `chase-backdrop.md`.
- **The backdrop never runs out in sight.** Its speed is tied to what is left of the strip: within a screen of its end it brakes softly to almost nothing, while the floor runs on - a far wall that barely crawls reads as a far wall. If the strip ends anyway, the chase ends with it (a guard, not a plan).
- **The pads stand in the world** and ride left with the floor. The pattern is built from hand-made chunks of three or four pads, each passable by design, and a chunk leading to the hatch's height comes every few seconds. Some pads carry a medkit from the start - only on the chunks that lead up. The carousels are dropped.
- **Three speeds:** the backdrop slowest, the fangs faster, the road fastest. The fangs are drawn behind the play layer.
- **The bunker drives the hero up and keeps him off its top.** The volley: two short barrels lob shells that land and roll left along the road, faster than the belt - the floor is no shelter. Then the hatch opens: for its first 0.5 s a column of hot exhaust goes straight up 2-3 cells; then it stands open and cooling for about 1.2 s - the window. Straight over the hatch is the column, so the hatch is struck from a pad above and to the side, as the pad drifts past - dodge, climb, catch the window.
- **The bunker by thirds:** one shell and a low column; two shells and a higher column; the shells hop to the height of a low pad, so the low pads are no shelter either.
- **The brake lurch** only in the bunker's first two thirds; the last third has the hopping shells and the leash.
- **No new art for the weapons.** The bunker, its lid and the mortar are in `tek-machine.mset`; the napalm shell, the drop, the splash and the soot, the bunker's shells, the column and the haze are drawn in code. The chase's only new art is the backdrop strip.
