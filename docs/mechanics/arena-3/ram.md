## The boss: TEK-R13, the Ram
- Named 2026-10-26: TEK-R13 ("Таран" in the game's Russian), the Ram - a TEK index as the tank's TEK-J02 and the cordon's TEK-Q6; R for the ram blade it leads with, 13 for the screen it holds.
- A big heavy TEK vehicle on two wheels, 169 by 128 units (see below), under the TEK shield - the double-barrel does not get through it. Drawn as a hull (Monsters.Hull: layers, wheels turned by how far the body has rolled).
- Only the turrets can be hit. They come out on top on a mast (up to three cells high, swaying - the 2026-10-08 idea). Kill one, the next comes out and shoots differently.
- Three turrets. A fourth only if the chase proves too short live (see Open).

### The machine, decided 2026-10-26 (over the text below where they disagree)
- **Size.** The hull art came out about 4 cells long by 3 tall; at 4 cells long it would be 97 units tall, and the hero's arc peaks at 100 - he would jump it. It is scaled by its height instead: 128 units tall, 169 long (5.3 cells). Its top stays at 224, so the pads of screen 13 stand as they are; it has less floor to drive on.
- **It never turns round.** Too big for a turn to read as anything but a jump cut. It faces left for good and drives back and forth, backward when it goes right. The art is mirrored once when the set is packed; the game draws it as it is (no `mirrors`). The wheels turn the other way when it backs.
- **The chase goes right, so the machine backs through it**, its ram toward the hero who runs after it. The mortar on the stern fires over the hull and the napalm falls behind the hero, as planned.
- **Contact wounds and shoves** (a new rule; no enemy did it before): any touch of the hull or a wheel takes a wound and throws the hero away from the machine's middle, up and back, with a short loss of control and a moment of mercy. It is the "crush" of 2026-10-08 made a rule, and it holds everywhere - the ram driving into him, the brake lurch, a fall onto it. The roof is out of reach (128 over the floor against a peak of 100), and a hero who drops on it from a pad is thrown off by the same rule.
- **The eye is the telegraph.** The machine has a face that always looks toward the hero in the chase, so the warnings live there: the eye blinks fast for half a second before the brake lurch (the brake lights face away from the hero in the chase - they only burn on braking, for the look), and blinks while a new head rises.
- **The mast.** Three telescopic sections out of the well on the roof; a head on the top of the third. It sways a little (the 2026-10-08 idea): life, not aim. What stands over the well's middle line is drawn over the hull; the rest is inside it.
- **Three heads, one freedom each** - the player tells them apart by what moves:
  - **Hunter** (turret 1): the mast stands, the barrel turns on its sleeve to any angle and follows the hero; the lens flares before a burst of three. No blind spot - only moving saves.
  - **Sweeper** (turret 2): four level barrels; the head rides the mast up and down to the row of the hero and sweeps it. It does not tilt: a pad over it is no refuge, because the mast at full height (three cells) lifts the barrels to a hero on the middle pad. It comes to the hero's row, not a random one, so standing still does not work. The warning: the barrels redden once the head has stopped on its row (`sweeperHeat` tinted by code), then the sweep. It cannot come down under its own roof (224): a hero on the floor is out of its reach and in the ram's - the machine drives at him. Then the sweeper **closes the ceiling** (decided 2026-10-26): it sinks to the bottom of its mast and sweeps the row just over his jump, so he may stand on the floor but not jump up to a pad while the row burns - he waits for the gap between sweeps with the ram coming.
  - **Bunker** (turret 3, the chase): stands low on the mast; two short barrels fire a volley that drops to the floor and rolls along it; the hatch on its top **breathes** - shut for the volley, open about 1.5 s after it to vent; it is hit only through the open hatch, from above. The honeycomb closes its sides and front.
- **A head turns** to face the hero by squashing to nothing across its socket and opening mirrored, in about 0.3 s - all three do it. The turn is a warning of its own: the head faced him, a volley is coming.
- **The hunter turns too** (decided 2026-10-26, over "the hunter needs no turn"): its barrel's sleeve is at the left edge of the head, so a barrel swung to the right would lie across the head and its lens, and an instant flip of the head reads cheap. So the barrel aims within its own half - from straight up to a little below level on the side the head faces - and past the vertical the head turns as the others do. The head turns only when the hero has stayed past the vertical by about 15 degrees for about 0.3 s, or a hero right over it would flap it to and fro; never in the middle of a burst. Stage 9.
- **The change of heads.** The killed head blows up, the mast sinks into the well empty and smoking, the eye blinks, the next head rises - about 1.5 s of breath that shows the progress.
- **The demo stand** of the art and these rules (a page of its own, not in the repository) runs on screen 13 with its pads, floor and backdrop; the numbers in it are first guesses.

### Act 1 - standing ground (turrets 1 and 2)
- The machine drives back and forth across the arena, stops, shoots, drives again; driving into the hero wounds and shoves him (decided 2026-10-08, made the contact rule 2026-10-26).
- Turret 1, the hunter: aimed bursts at the hero - teaches to move.
- Turret 2, the sweeper: rides the mast to the hero's row and sweeps it, with the red warning on the barrels first - teaches to change height.

**Decided 2026-10-27 for turret 1, over the text above where they disagree:**
- **No safe place, no quiet harbour.** The first stage is the simplest of the fight, but not a shooting range: the player must feel the finale of 3.0 from it.
- **Two modes by the hero's height.** Low - where the ram can really crush him: the road, and a jump off it up to the height of the low pads, not standing on them - the machine presses: it thinks faster and drives all the way to the wall on his side, and the gun holds its fire (one threat at a time). Higher - on the pads, out of the ram's reach but not the gun's - it roams in short legs, the barrel looks at the hero and fires.
- **The mast does not hide for a press.** It goes down only for the change of heads: a gun that hid while the machine pressed would open the way up too easily.
- **Only the eye takes a hit.** The hunter's lens is the target; bullets pass the mast, the head and the barrel.
- **About 100 lives**, more on the harder grades (as the monsters': times 1.5 and 2) - tuned live.
- **Bursts with rests between them.** A hero must be hurt by his own mistake, not because there was no way out: every burst comes after the lens has burned red, and a rest follows it.
- **Harder by the thirds of its lives** (taken over a sniper's single pattern and a hose's): three shots where he stood; three where he will be if he keeps going; a hose of six that keeps following him, and a harder press.

**Decided 2026-10-28 for turret 2, over the text above where they disagree:**
- **The target is the whole barrel pack** - the four barrels, not an eye. Bullets pass the mast and the head.
- **Shot only from over the roof.** Two small plates of the TEK honeycomb under the barrels (`Game.Shield`) take every bullet that comes from under the roof line (224): a hit flares the cells and does nothing. A hero on the road cannot hurt it - he has to climb. With the closed ceiling it is one rule: the floor is the ram's and does no damage.
- **About 140 lives**, times 1.5 and 2 on the harder grades (the target is bigger than the hunter's eye) - tuned live.
- **Fire by the thirds of its lives**, as the hunter's: first third - one sweep of the hero's row; second - the fork: his row, then at once the row next to it, the one he is most likely to jump to; last third - the ladder: the head rides the mast while it fires, so the line of bullets climbs or sinks and has to be jumped through or let pass over. The red barrels before every sweep stay.

**Decided 2026-10-28 for both heads:**
- **They short out from half their lives.** Small bolts on the head (`TLightning`, as the tank's), none above half; under half, more often the fewer lives are left. The hunter's smoke at its last third stays. Stage 10b brings it to the hunter of 3.0.76 too.
- **Medkits on the thirds.** A medkit comes as a head's row of lives passes into its next third - two a head, four over turrets 1 and 2. None at the change of heads: the breath between heads is the reward. The hunter's go onto a high pad (`s13-pad-2` or `-3`), in its sights; the sweeper's onto the road at the wall away from the Ram, on the ram's ground under the closed ceiling - a reward inside the current threat both times. A death restarts the fight, and they with it.
- **The shuttle drops them.** A small capsule drawn in code (as the life sphere): it falls from the top of the screen with a light, opens on the ground or the pad and leaves the medkit. No line of the shuttle - the capsule says it.

**Decided 2026-10-28, the blast of the sweeper** (seen on a demo stand, accepted):

### Stage 8a as built (3.0.74)
Screen 13 of level 2 stands as data and art; the machine is 8b. Two words of code: the music hands over after a fade-out, and a door may be sealed for good.

- **The music** (3.0.74): what the vocabulary had - `music` with `fade` brings a track in from silence but cuts the one before (`PlayMusic` halts it); the scene cue `music` sets the volume of the track that plays and changes no track, so a scene ending at 0 would leave the next track mute; and a scene fading the track with an event changing it after a delay would remember the new track late - a death in the fade would restart into the old one. What was added: `"fadeOut"` (seconds) on `music` (`FadeOutSeconds`, Levels.Events; `SEventBadFadeOut` below 0). The track that plays fades out (`TSoundBank.HandOverMusic`, `Mix_FadeOutMusic`), the new one waits (`TQueuedTrack`) and starts, with its own fade-in, the tick the mixer is silent (`TSoundBank.Tick`, called by the game every tick in every state, so a fade runs on under the menu). The game remembers the new track at once (`ChangeMusic`), so a restart at any moment plays it. A track already playing is left alone: the restart starts the boss track, the re-armed event asks for it again and nothing happens. `PlayMusic` and `StopMusic` drop a waiting track. Tests: three in `Tests.Levels.Events` - the fade-out read beside the fade-in, none by default, below 0 refused.
- **The boss track:** `boss3.ogg` (the numbering of the music folder: boss2 is the gravel trial).
